Cassette swap

11–28 versus 11–34, on a 50/34

The useful question is not “how many speeds is it.” It is which gears get easier, which stay put, and which new cogs land on a rollout you can already reach with the other chainring.

The two tooth lists

These are common published 11-cog sequences, not a claim that every cassette with those end teeth uses them. Edit the calculator if the cluster in your hand differs.

  • 11–28: 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 17, 19, 21, 23, 25, 28
  • 11–34: 11, 13, 15, 17, 19, 21, 23, 25, 27, 30, 34

Wheel: 700×28c, bead seat 622 mm, tire height equal to the labeled width, rolling diameter 678 mm. Chainrings: 50 and 34. Cadence for the speeds below: 90 rpm.

What changes

Lowest and highest gears. The 11-tooth cog is on both cassettes, so the hardest gear does not move.
EasiestGear inches90 rpmHardest
11–2834×2832.4 in8.7 mph50×11, 121.3 in
11–3434×3426.7 in7.1 mph50×11, 121.3 in

26.7 is 17.6% lower than 32.4. That is the entire climbing change if you stay on the 34-tooth ring: about 2.13 meters per stroke instead of 2.59. The 50×11 is still 9.68 meters and 32.5 mph at 90 rpm. Range goes from 3.74× to 4.55× because the bottom moved and the top did not.

Most of the “new” cassette is not new

Each cassette has 11 cogs and two chainrings, so 22 combinations. Eighteen of the 11–34 combinations sit within 3% of some gear the 11–28 already provides. Four do not:

  • 34×34 is 17.6% easier than the nearest gear you have, 34×28.
  • 34×30 is 6.7% easier than that same 34×28.
  • 34×27 is 3.7% harder than 34×28. It is a small step the other way, just outside the 3% band. It is not an easier climb.
  • 50×27 is 3.5% harder than 34×19. It fills a slightly different spot on the big ring. It does not extend the top or the bottom.

A 3% band is tighter than a normal one-tooth jump. Eleven to twelve teeth is about a 9% change. “Within 3%” means almost the same rollout, the kind of overlap you get when 50×16 and 34×11 describe nearly the same gear.

The derailleur has to swallow a wider cassette

Rear tooth span is the biggest cog minus the smallest. The 11–28 spans 17 teeth. The 11–34 spans 23. Add the 16 teeth between a 50 and a 34 and the total is 33 teeth for the 11–28 setup and 39 for the 11–34. That sum is the usual planning figure for how much chain a rear derailleur must take up. It is arithmetic. It is not a statement that a particular derailleur will shift either cassette. Read the capacity number the maker publishes for the derailleur you have, and check the largest cog it allows, before you buy.

The same comparison on your wheel, including a 1× or a third chainring, is the chart on the calculator. Clear the second cassette if you only want one column of gears.
