Gear inches

Ratio times wheel diameter. On this example, 50×11 is 121.3 in and 34×28 is 32.4 in.

Rollout

Distance per pedal stroke. That 50×11 moves 9.68 meters. A measured rollout beats the tire estimate.

Speed

Rollout times cadence. 50×11 at 90 rpm is 32.5 mph, if the tire is not slipping.

The low gear gets 17.6% easier. The top gear does not change. Most steps are ones you already have.

Wheel size

A wider tire on the same rim is a taller gear. A 29×2.4 and a 700×28c do not share a gear inch.

What this is not

Not a fit tool, not a training plan, and not a promise that a derailleur will shift the span.

Questions riders ask before a cassette swap

What is a gear inch?

Gear inches are the gear ratio multiplied by the wheel’s rolling diameter in inches. A higher number moves the bike farther per pedal stroke. The gear inches guide works a 50×11 by hand.

Does a bigger tire change the gear?

Yes. The calculator adds twice the tire width to the rim’s bead-seat diameter. A wider tire raises every gear inch on that bike. See how wheel size shifts the chart.

Will an 11–34 be easier than an 11–28?

With the same small chainring, the bigger cog is a lower gear. On the loaded example it is 17.6% easier, and the 50×11 top gear stays put. The comparison page shows which other cogs are new and which duplicate a gear you can already shift to. It does not say whether you need the easier gear.

Do you store what I type?

The numbers stay in this browser. Cogspan has no account and no server-side calculator. A local storage entry named cogspan.v1 remembers the last wheel, tooth counts, cadence, and unit on this device. Nothing is sent anywhere. Details are on the privacy page.
