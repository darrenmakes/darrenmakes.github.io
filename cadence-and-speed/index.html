Cadence

Speed is rollout times cadence

Once you know how far one pedal stroke goes, speed is that length times how many strokes you make in a minute. The calculator is doing that and nothing more athletic than that.

The formula

km/h = rollout (m) × cadence (rpm) × 60 ÷ 1000

Miles per hour are that result divided by 1.609344. A 9.68 m rollout at 90 rpm is 9.68 × 90 × 0.06 = 52.3 km/h, or 32.5 mph.

One gear, four cadences

The gear is 50×11 on a 700×28c tire with the height-factor-1 estimate (678 mm, 9.68 m of rollout). Only the cadence changes.

The same wheel’s easiest gear in the loaded road example, 34×28, is 2.59 m of rollout: 8.7 mph (14.0 km/h) at 90 rpm. That is why a cassette swap is a speed question and a climbing question at once. The 11–28 versus 11–34 comparison keeps the top end and lowers the bottom.

What the number leaves out

  • Wind, grade, and braking. The figure is the speed of the contact patch at a steady cadence, not a prediction for a ride.
  • Coasting. Cadence is zero while the bike keeps moving. The formula does not describe that.
  • Tire slip and skids. The math assumes the tire rolls the distance it is supposed to roll.
  • A recommended cadence. Cogspan will not tell you what to pedal. Type the cadence you actually use, or try a few, and read the speed that gear produces at that rate.

If you want the chart in km/h, switch the unit on the calculator. The gear inches and the rollout stay in the same units either way.
