Wheel diameter

The tire is part of the gear

Tooth counts do not know what wheel they are on. Gear inches do. A wider tire on the same rim is a slightly taller gear. A 29×2.4 and a 700×28c share a bead-seat size and still do not share a gear inch.

Bead seat, then tire

ISO bead-seat diameter is the rim diameter where the tire bead sits. It is the number that stays still when you change tires:

  • 700c and 29 inch: 622 mm
  • 650b and 27.5 inch: 584 mm
  • 26 inch, the common MTB size: 559 mm
  • The older 27 inch road rim: 630 mm

rolling diameter ≈ bead seat + 2 × labeled width × height factor

Height factor defaults to 1. Drop it if you know the mounted tire is shorter than the label, or replace the whole estimate with a measured rollout.

Height factor is 1. Speed is 90 rpm. The 34×28 column is there so the easy gear moves with the wheel too.

Going from a 23 mm tire to a 32 mm tire on a 700c rim, without touching the cassette, raises the 50×11 from 119.5 to 122.8 gear inches. That is a small change next to a cassette swap, and it is still a change. The 29×2.4 estimate is 133.1, about 10% taller than the 700×28c. A road cassette chart copied onto a 29er without resizing the wheel will overstate how easy the climbs are.

The matching 650b × 47 and 700×28c rows are an artifact of the height-factor-1 assumption: 584 + 2×47 and 622 + 2×28 are both 678. Real casings are not that neat. If you are choosing between those two wheels, measure both rollouts rather than trusting the tie.

A 1× chainring shifts the block. It does not change the spread.

On the 29×2.4 estimate, a 32-tooth ring with a 10–52 cassette runs from 18.0 gear inches (32×52, 1.44 m, 4.3 mph at 80 rpm) to 93.7 gear inches (32×10, 22.3 mph at 80 rpm). The range is 52 ÷ 10 = 5.20×. A 30-tooth ring on the same cassette is 16.9 to 87.9 gear inches. The range is still 5.20×. The smaller ring slides the whole ladder down. It does not add a cog.

A 10–51 is 5.10× on any chainring, and a 34-tooth ring slides the ladder the other way. The 1× chainring page puts 30, 32, and 34 teeth on both cassettes. Cassette swaps on a road double are charted separately: 11–28 versus 11–34, 11–32 versus 11–34, and 10–33 versus 10–36.
