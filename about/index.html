About

A gear chart for the cassette decision

Cogspan is a free calculator for riders and mechanics who want gear inches, rollout, and speed before they buy a cassette, a chainring, or a different wheel. This page was published with the first version of the site on October 7, 2026.

Who it is for

You already know the teeth on the bike. You want to know whether an 11–34 actually adds an easier gear — from an 11–28 or from an 11–32 — or whether a 30-tooth ring on a 1× only slides the same spread downward. A 12-cog road swap is the 10–33 versus 10–36 page. The chart is meant to be used on a phone in a shop aisle or at a workstand.

Definitions

Gear inches are the gear ratio times the rolling diameter in inches. Rollout, also called development, is the distance traveled in one pedal revolution. Gain ratio, shown only if you enter a crank length, is wheel radius divided by crank length, times the gear ratio. Speed is rollout times cadence, converted to mph or km/h.

Those definitions have been in workshop use for a long time. Sheldon Brown’s gear calculator is the long-standing reference on the web. Cogspan is independent of that site. It does not copy it. The focus here is a readable comparison of two cassettes, a measured-rollout override, and a page that works on a small screen.

Cassette presets are common published tooth sequences. They are not a catalog, and they are not affiliated with any drivetrain maker. If a tooth on the preset does not match the cassette in your hand, change the number.

What Cogspan will not tell you

  • It is not a bike fit. Crank length is only there for gain ratio.
  • It is not a training plan and it does not recommend a cadence.
  • It is not a derailleur compatibility certificate. The tooth-span line is biggest cog minus smallest cog, plus the gap between chainrings. Check the derailleur maker’s own capacity and maximum-cog figures before you buy.
  • Tire height defaults to the labeled width. That is an estimate. A measured rollout replaces it.

How the pages stay honest

There is no account, no review score, and no count of users. The worked examples in the guides use the same formulas as the calculator, checked by an automated test in this project. If a guide and the chart ever disagree, the chart and the test are the source of truth.

There is no contact form, because the site does not collect messages. The privacy page says what stays on your device.
